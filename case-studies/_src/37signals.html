01The challenge

37signals' annual cloud bill had reached about $3.2 million [1]. Its products had mature, predictable traffic, so it was paying a premium for elasticity it rarely used, and the promised savings in operations effort hadn't materialised.

02What 37signals did

  • Worked out the real unit cost of running its steady workloads, and compared renting with owning.
  • Bought hardware for the predictable baseline and moved its applications out of the cloud during 2023 [1].
  • Kept the same team. The move was done without adding anyone to the operations team [1].
  • Finished the job. It later moved its remaining storage off the cloud as its storage contract ended in 2025 [2].

03The results

37signals projects savings of about $10 million over five years [1], and reports the move was done without growing its operations team.

The lesson: the cloud is a pricing model, not a default. Know what each workload costs you, and pay for elasticity only where you actually use it.

04At your scale: a 90-day plan

Most mid-size companies shouldn't buy servers: 37signals had a steady, well-understood workload and an experienced ops team. Apply the same discipline inside the cloud first.

Days 1–30 · See the spendDays 31–60 · Cut the wasteDays 61–90 · Commit and govern
  • Tag every resource by team, product and environment
  • Attribute 90%+ of the bill to an owner
  • Work out cost per customer or per transaction
  • Right-size over-provisioned instances and databases
  • Schedule non-production to switch off out of hours
  • Delete idle volumes, snapshots and load balancers
  • Buy Savings Plans, reservations or committed-use discounts only for steady usage
  • Budgets and anomaly alerts routed to owners
  • A monthly FinOps review; revisit repatriation only for truly flat workloads

05Readiness checklist

  • You know last month's bill by team
  • Every resource has an owner tag
  • You know your cost per customer
  • Non-production shuts down at night
  • Commitments cover only steady usage
  • Anomaly alerts reach the owning team
  • Spend is reviewed monthly
  • You know which workloads are truly flat
