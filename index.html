      Case studies

      Lessons from the teams that set the standard

      Four well-documented DevOps stories, what actually made them work, and how we get you the same result at your size and budget.

      Public case studies, with sources on each card. These companies are not eXommerce clients.

      Etsy · online marketplace

      Deploying 50+ times a day without fear

      50+deploys a day
      14,000+test-suite runs a day
      What worked
      Tiny changes shipped continuously, a one-click deploy tool, feature flags to launch code dark, and a rule that everything that can be graphed is graphed.

      At your scaleYou don't need Etsy's in-house tooling. Managed CI (GitHub Actions or GitLab), a feature-flag service and one shared dashboard give a 5–50 person team the same habit: small, boring, frequent releases.

      Source: InfoQ, Etsy at QCon London 2014

      Netflix · streaming

      Staying up when the cloud went down

      Apr 2011major AWS US-East outage
      Stayed upNetflix kept streaming
      What worked
      Assuming failure is normal: running across several availability zones, degrading gracefully, and Chaos Monkey switching off production servers on purpose so recovery was routine.

      At your scaleNo need to build a chaos army. Run in two or more zones behind health checks and autoscaling, set an SLO for the journey that pays the bills, and hold a quarterly game day where we switch something off on purpose.

      Source: Netflix Technology Blog, April 2011

      Capital One · banking

      A regulated bank that ships many times a day

      3 months → minutesto build a dev environment
      −50%critical incident resolution time
      What worked
      Treating automation as the control: security and compliance built into the platform, which took releases from monthly or quarterly to many times a day while it exited all eight data centres.

      At your scaleYou aren't rebuilding 2,000 apps over eight years. A landing zone built as code, policy guardrails and a pipeline that records audit evidence give one product team the same pattern in weeks, not years.

      Source: AWS case study: Capital One

      37signals · SaaS (Basecamp, HEY)

      Rethinking a $3.2M-a-year cloud bill

      $3.2Myearly cloud bill before
      ~$10Mprojected 5-year savings
      What worked
      Knowing the real unit cost of steady, predictable workloads, then moving them onto owned hardware without adding anyone to the operations team.

      At your scaleMost mid-size companies shouldn't buy servers. Apply the same discipline in the cloud: tag every cost, right-size, commit only on steady usage, and weigh leaving the cloud only for the workloads that are truly flat.

      Source: 37signals, Leaving the Cloud
